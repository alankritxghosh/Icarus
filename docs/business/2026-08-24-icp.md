## The finding that has to come first

**Every prospect list this company has built was selected on a property of the
EVIDENCE, not on a property of the BUYER.**

`sales/roll_up.py` produces 127 verified owners across 102 repos, and the
qualification is: a real human with merge rights, on a repo with a usable
closed-pull-request history. That is a filter for *where Icarus demos well*.

It is not a filter for *who has the pain*. Those came apart and nobody noticed,
because a repo with rich refused-PR history makes a beautiful demo and also
tends to be a healthy open-source project whose maintainers are unpaid.

**~104 sends, 2 human replies, 1 positive.** The single positive was **Richie
McIlroy, CapSoftware/Cap** — and it arrived by X DM, on the fallback channel,
because Cap publishes only a noreply address. Cap is a small commercial startup
with an open-source product. That is one data point and it is the best one we
have.

---

## The pain, stated mechanically

Icarus answers *why* from recorded history, and says so when nothing was
recorded. The value of that is a function of three things, none of which is
"has interesting PRs":

1. **Distance between the person asking and the person who decided.** Zero when
   the decider is sitting next to you. Large when they left, or when there are
   enough people that nobody tracks everything.
2. **Age and volume of recorded history.** A six-month-old repo has little to
   remember.
3. **Cost of getting it wrong.** Re-litigating a settled decision is annoying at
   3 people and expensive at 30.

**Team size is the crude proxy for (1), and it points somewhere the current
list does not.** A 3-person startup, our stated prospect profile, has close to
zero distance — everyone remembers everything. That is the segment where this
product is *least* needed, and it is the segment 102 of the outreach repos sit in.

## Candidate segments

### A. Open-source maintainers
**For:** where every demo asset comes from; the evidence is public so we can
prove value before contact; the closed-unmerged-PR insight is sharpest here.
**Against:** **they do not buy.** No budget, no procurement, and the pain is
partly solved by the fact that the history is public and searchable. Our own
data: 104 sends into roughly this segment produced one "very interesting!".
**Verdict:** a distribution channel and a proving ground. Not a buyer.

### B. Seed-to-Series-A startups, 10-40 engineers
**For:** distance is real — people have joined after the founding decisions and
the deciders are busy, not gone. History exists (2-4 years). Budget exists and
one person can approve it. Private repos, which is the paid path.
**Against:** unverified. **We have never spoken to one.** Cap is adjacent and is
the only signal.
**Verdict:** the most likely buyer on the mechanics. Entirely untested.

### C. Enterprise platform teams
**For:** the pain is largest and best funded; turnover is real; "why does this
exist" is a daily cost.
**Against:** SOC 2, ISO 27001 and BAAs are named in `docs/VISION.md` as *not yet
pursued*. Sales cycles measured in quarters. A solo founder cannot service it.
**Verdict:** where this goes, not where it starts.

### D. Coding-agent users, sold as agent infrastructure
**For:** the MCP surface is shipped and this is the fastest-moving market. The
wedge is specific: an agent is structurally blind to a refused pull request.
**Against:** **the evidence for it is thin and we now know it is thinner than
claimed.** C2's 4-of-4 rests on four tasks, one session, one repo, and 7b
(2026-08-24) got 0 of 2 on an unseen repo — inconclusive by design, but it did
not reproduce. Selling on this before it is re-measured would be selling an
unverified claim.
**Verdict:** the most exciting story and the least substantiated. Sequence it
behind a clean re-run.

---

## Recommendation

**B, with A as the channel.** Sell to seed-to-Series-A teams of roughly 10-40
engineers; keep using open source to demo, to publish, and to be found.

The reasoning is that B is the only segment where all three drivers of the pain
are simultaneously non-zero and someone can sign. A has the evidence and no
money; C has the money and needs compliance we do not have; D has the story and
not the proof.

**This recommendation is not yet a decision, and it should not become one on my
reasoning.** It rests on a mechanical argument about where the pain lives, plus
n=1. What would make it a decision is below, and it is cheap.

---

## What would settle it — five conversations, not a survey

Talk to **five engineering leads at B-shaped companies** and ask only:

1. When you need to know why something in your codebase is the way it is, what
   do you do today, and when did you last do it?
2. What did it cost the last time nobody remembered?
3. Who would have to approve a tool that reads your private repository?
4. What would you need to see before connecting it?
5. Has anyone on the team shipped something someone else had already tried?

**Do not describe the product until all five are answered.** The failure mode is
a demo that produces enthusiasm and no information.

Start with Richie McIlroy at Cap — he already said "very interesting!", got one
follow-up, and was then written off inside a number that turned out to be
wrong. He is the warmest contact this company has and he is not a stranger.

## Open, and honestly unknown

- Whether 10-40 engineers is the right band, or whether it is 30-100. The
  mechanics argue "when the first person who decided has left", and nobody has
  measured when that happens.
- Whether the buyer is the engineering lead, the platform lead, or a founder.
- Whether B companies feel this as a *problem to spend on* or as weather.
