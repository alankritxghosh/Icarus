# Four-week X and LinkedIn distribution plan

Prepared 2026-08-12. Nothing is scheduled or published. "Post" below means a
drafted slot that still requires Alankrit's approval at publishing time.

## Objective and audience

First objective: establish correct awareness of what Icarus is. Second
objective: create qualified conversations with engineering leaders and
technically demanding early users who will test it on a real repository.

Primary audience:

- X: coding-agent power users, developer-tool builders, and technical voices.
- LinkedIn: heads of engineering, developer-productivity/platform leaders,
  CTOs, and technical founders responsible for team adoption.

The conversion is a real-repository trial or a blunt technical conversation,
not an impression, like, install, or mailing-list signup.

## Operating constraints

- Assume zero brand awareness. Every standalone post and first-touch message
  defines Icarus before presenting a claim, feature, or ask.
- Use one consistent plain-language definition: "engineering memory for
  software teams and their coding agents; it reads a repository's GitHub
  record and returns cited context or an honest unknown before someone changes
  the code."
- Repeat the category sentence in later standalone posts until audience
  responses show unaided understanding. Do not assume someone saw week 1.
- Two original posts per platform per week is the ceiling, not a quota.
- The opening post or DM contains one claim and one ask.
- Use real product captures only. Generated visuals may be atmosphere, never a
  substitute for product evidence.
- Reply to comments while the post is active. Do not automate replies.
- Do not paste identical copy across X and LinkedIn.
- No external link in the body of the first demo post. Provide it when asked.
- Do not publish or message without explicit authorization.

| Day | X | LinkedIn | Engagement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tuesday | `panel_refusal.png` and the line "No one wrote this down" | None | Ask which undocumented decision their team keeps re-explaining |

The memory-loop post must preserve the control boundary: a human supplies the
rationale, Icarus creates no autonomous decision, GitHub review remains the
approval path, and the record becomes citable only after merge and re-indexing.

## Daily engagement routine

Three days per week, 20 minutes maximum:

1. Respond to substantive comments on Icarus posts.
2. Leave 3 useful comments on target-account posts. Add an observation or
   question; never write "great post" or inject Icarus where irrelevant.
3. Record pain-point language verbatim in the weekly review.
4. Stop. Do not let reply activity consume the product-building day.

## Measurement sheet

Record one row per post or outreach wave:

| Date | Platform | Asset/theme | Impressions | Profile visits | Substantive comments | Correctly described Icarus? | Qualified conversations | Trial/demo asks | Notes |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|

A qualified conversation is with a target user or buyer and discusses a real
repository, team workflow, context loss, trust boundary, or adoption concern.
"Interesting" and generic praise do not count.

Also record an awareness check: can the person describe Icarus as engineering
memory shared by people and coding agents without being corrected? Questions
such as "is this a coding agent?", "is this only the voice overlay?", or "is
this another code search tool?" indicate that the introduction is still
unclear, even if the post received engagement.

### Four-week decision rule

- Success: at least 3 qualified conversations or 2 real-repository trial asks.
- Signal but not proof: 1 to 2 qualified conversations. Change the hook or ask,
  not the information volume, and run one more narrow cycle.
- No signal: zero qualified conversations after all four weeks and the planned
  targeted engagement. Stop increasing content volume. Test a warmer channel
  or a different ask.

Impressions, follows, and likes diagnose reach. They do not override the
qualified-conversation outcome.

## Hacker News decision

Use Hacker News as a lead and language source first, not as a routine
distribution channel.

Reasoning:

- The unresolved problem is finding people with the pain, not maximizing raw
  launch traffic.
- Comments on relevant agent, codebase, onboarding, and institutional-memory
  discussions reveal concrete pain language and people willing to discuss it.
- Repeated submissions without a substantive new release risk producing noisy
  traffic and weak learning.

For this four-week cycle, do not establish an HN cross-post routine. Collect
public problem statements and possible conversation targets from relevant
threads. Submit Icarus only when there is a genuine launch artifact: a public
demo, measured Agent Mode result, or meaningful product release. At that point,
write an HN-native technical explanation rather than reposting X copy.
