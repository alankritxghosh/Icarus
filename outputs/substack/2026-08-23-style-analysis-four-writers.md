# Style analysis — Koe, Naval, Kamath, Shamani

Captured 2026-08-23 by reading actual post bodies on Substack, not titles or
recall. Engagement figures are Substack's own like/restack/comment counts as
displayed at capture. All four are on Substack; two of them do their real
writing elsewhere and mirror it in.

---

## 1. Dan Koe — `letters.thedankoe.com`, "future/proof"

**Read:** *Strategy vs tactics: How to actually get ahead of 99% of people*.
276 block elements. Post engagement 822 to 1.8k; his Notes run far higher, one
at 7.5k.

**Structure, beat by beat:**
1. A sweeping claim about everyone. Present tense, no qualifier.
2. A fake-precise statistic used rhetorically, not evidentially (99.99%).
3. The diagnosis of what the reader is doing wrong.
4. Rhetorical questions as paragraph breaks, stacked two and three deep.
5. **The self-implication turn.** He was in the same trap 6 years ago and still
   falls in. This is the load-bearing move — it converts an accusation into
   company, and it arrives exactly when the reader is bristling.
6. Anaphora block: three parallel short sentences with an identical refrain.
7. An explicit roadmap paragraph naming what the article will cover.
8. A warning that it is long, framed as a feature.
9. Roman-numeral sections whose headers are complete thesis sentences.

**Mechanics:** paragraphs are one to two sentences, frequently one line. Second
person throughout, and accusatory rather than inclusive. No sources, no
citations, no links out. Typos ship uncorrected ("Their obsessed with tactics") —
velocity is visibly prioritised over polish. A product ladder sits in the footer.

**What actually does the work:** he tells the reader something unflattering
about themselves, then immediately confesses to it. Neither half works alone.

## 2. Naval Ravikant — `nav.al`, Substack "Live in the Future"

**Read:** archive titles plus the AI Industrial Revolution executive brief.
Substack engagement 167 to 270 — by far the lowest of the four, and the number
is the least meaningful thing about him.

**Titles are two to five words.** Imperative or bare declarative: *Sell the
Truth*, *Curate People*, *Waste Tokens Save Time*, *In the Arena*, *A Return to
Code*.

**Structure:** the current Substack output is numbered executive briefs of
podcast episodes. Each numbered item is a compressed, unhedged thesis. No "I
think", no evidence, no citation, no worked example.

**The unit of composition is the sentence, not the paragraph.** Nothing depends
on what came before it, which is what makes any line liftable.

**The strategically important finding, and it is not about prose.** Searching
Substack for his name returns mostly *other people writing about him* — quote
compilations, book summaries, lesson lists, at 219, 238, 286, 319, 488 and up.
Several outperform his own posts. His style is optimised for extraction: he
writes in units other people can steal cleanly, and his distribution is largely
other people's accounts. That is a different game from newsletter writing and it
requires standing he already has. **Not transferable at 14 followers.**

## 3. Nithin Kamath — `nithinkamath.substack.com`

**Read:** *What will the future of trading and investing look like?* — **11
block elements**, the shortest thing in this set by an order of magnitude.
Engagement 40 to 947 across recent posts.

**Structure:**
1. A modest opener that concedes the obvious ("on the surface, a lot has
   changed").
2. A concrete operational fact about how the thing actually works underneath.
3. The question the company is genuinely asking itself, stated as a question.
4. Live uncertainty named as uncertainty, twice, without embarrassment.
5. **The negative space.** What they are *not* looking for, listed concretely —
   dashboards, visualisation tools, repackaged public data.
6. A direct ask, and a link to the longer version elsewhere.

**Mechanics:** first person plural. No rhetorical questions aimed at the reader.
No second-person accusation anywhere. No self-help register at all. When he has
numbers he uses exact ones (the NSE note carries ₹10,300 crore profit, ₹8,660
crore dividends, 84% payout). Register is a founder thinking out loud in public,
not a teacher instructing.

**What does the work:** admitted uncertainty plus specificity. He is credible
because he says what he does not know, and then is exact about what he does.

## 4. Raj Shamani — `rajshamani.substack.com`

**Read:** *Why society requires hypocrisy to function*. 65 block elements.
Engagement 294 to 993, the most consistent performance in the set.

**Every title is a "Why" question.** Why do your parents have such a hard time
understanding you. Why does the young generation hate their reality. Why most
young people don't know what they want to do with their lives.

**Structure:**
1. **A micro-scene the reader has lived through**, in the first line. The
   "how are you doing" exchange. Not a claim — a moment.
2. A question about that moment aimed at the reader.
3. The uncomfortable reframe: the ordinary thing you do is technically the bad
   thing you condemn.
4. An explicit concession to the opposing view before continuing.
5. **Escalating scale.** Two-person exchange, then the household, then society,
   then moral values. Same argument, four altitudes.
6. Section headers that are full thesis sentences.

**Mechanics:** second person, but inclusive — "we", "you and I both". Long
multi-clause sentences, noticeably more literary than Koe. **Zero numbers, zero
citations, zero sources anywhere in the piece.** The authority is entirely in
the observation.

## The comparison that matters

| | Koe | Naval | Kamath | Shamani |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Length | very long | very short | short | medium |
| Person | you (accusatory) | none | we | we / you (inclusive) |
| Evidence | none | none | exact numbers | none |
| Admits uncertainty | no | no | **yes, repeatedly** | partially |
| Opens on | a claim about everyone | an aphorism | a concrete mechanism | a lived scene |
| Sells | courses | standing | nothing directly | nothing directly |

**Three of the four write advice with no evidence.** That is not a criticism of
them; it is the genre. Their authority comes from confidence, standing, or
observation quality.

**That genre is unavailable here, and imitating it would be actively bad.**
Icarus's entire position is that it does not assert what it cannot show. A
Substack written in Koe's register — sweeping claims about what everyone is
doing wrong, no sources — contradicts the product on the page where someone
first meets it.

**Kamath is the only one whose form fits**, and the overlap is not superficial:
short, first-person-plural, exact when exact numbers exist, explicit about what
is unknown, defines the negative space, and ends on a real ask. That is already
close to how the two drafted articles read.

**Two things worth stealing that do not cost anything:**

- **Shamani's opener.** Both current drafts open on a claim. Opening instead on
  a scene the reader has lived — the moment an agent confidently tells you a
  bug is fixed — costs nothing and is not in tension with the evidence.
- **Koe's self-implication.** Article 2 already does this by accident: the
  protocol was broken by its own author one day after being written. It is the
  strongest passage in either draft, and now there is a name for why.

**One thing worth refusing:** Koe's roadmap paragraph and length-warning. They
work for a 120,000-subscriber list where the reader has already decided. On a
new publication they read as throat-clearing before the argument.

# STANDING RULE — every headline is a "Why" (set 2026-08-23, Alankrit)

**Every article title opens with "Why" and answers a question the reader
already has.**

Taken from the strongest-performing pattern in this analysis. Shamani's titles
are *all* "Why" constructions and his engagement is the most consistent of the
four (294 to 993 across recent posts). None of the other three do this.

## The pattern, precisely

It is not "put a question mark on it". Two of Shamani's do not have one — *Why
most young people don't know what they want to do with their lives* is a phrase,
not a question. The rule is:

**Why + a claim the reader recognises as true about themselves or their world.**

The title is a promise that a felt thing has an explanation. The reader arrives
already holding the question.

## What this rules out

- **A title that names the artifact.** "I built a hallucination detector" is a
  fact about the author. Nobody was wondering.
- **A title that states the conclusion.** "Verification became my whole job"
  closes the loop before the reader opens it.
- **"How" and "What".** How-titles promise instruction, which puts us in Koe's
  genre where we cannot win. Why-titles promise an explanation, which is what we
  actually have evidence for.

## Applied to the three current drafts

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| Verification became my whole job | **Why you spend your day checking instead of building** |
| I built a hallucination detector. It scored the one lie higher than the truth. | **Why you trust a made-up answer more than a true one** |
| Every rule in my experiment protocol exists because I broke it first | **Why the rules you write down are the ones you break** |

All three now have **you** as the grammatical subject or direct addressee, in
present tense, naming a condition rather than reporting an event.

## The second turn of the screw — make the claim about the reader

The first pass produced *Why checking the work became the work*. It obeys the
rule and is still weak, because the subject is the work and the tense is
retrospective. It reports something that happened to the author.

Shamani's titles name a **predicament the reader is currently inside**: parents
who do not understand you, a generation that hates its reality, not knowing what
to do with your life. Present tense, second person, and a condition rather than
an event.

Applied here, the predicament is: you were given a machine that writes code and
your day got worse in a way you cannot name. Hence *Why you spend your day
checking instead of building*.

**The test to run on any title that already passes the Why rule:** who is the
subject of the sentence. If it is the work, the product, the experiment or the
author, turn it until it is the reader.

## Where it does not apply

Section headers inside a piece. Shamani's internal headers are full thesis
*sentences*, not questions, and stacking questions inside a question reads as a
listicle. Keep the internal headers declarative.
