## The band, and why it is not the real criterion

You asked for 1k–10k followers, active, good engagement. Right instinct, but the
mechanism underneath it matters more than the number:

**Reply reach comes from the parent post's reach. Reply *visibility* comes from
how crowded the replies are.** Those pull in opposite directions.

| Parent size | What you get | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| 30k+ followers | Large impression pool | Your reply is #47 in the thread. Author never sees it. |
| **1k–10k** | Smaller pool | **You are #3. The author reads it and often replies.** |
| <500 | Almost no pool | Not worth the slot |

The 1k–10k band is right for **relationships** — the author notices you, and
that is what turns into a follow and eventually a conversation. Big accounts are
right for **impressions**. Run both, and know which you are buying each time.

**Screen on engagement RATE, not follower count:** a 3k account pulling 40 likes
is a live audience; a 30k account pulling 12 is a dead one you are shouting into.

## How to add to this list yourself — the screen, in order

1. **Find the post first, not the account.** Run the searches in
   `reply-targets-2026-08-22.md`, sorted by Latest.
2. **Read the last 5 posts before anything else.** Not the bio, not the follower
   count — both describe an account at its best and neither tells you whether it
   still posts, in what language, or about what. This step cost 4 of 5 additions
   on 2026-08-23 by being skipped. If the last post is months old, or the subject
   is nothing you have material for, stop here.
3. **Then the profile numbers:** follower count, posts-per-day, and whether those
   5 posts are their own thinking or recycled threads.
4. **Compute the crude rate:** likes on recent posts ÷ followers. Under ~0.5%
   the audience is asleep — skip regardless of size.
5. **Check the ratio.** Following ≫ followers means the audience was farmed, not
   earned. Their followers will not convert for you either.
6. **Check they reply to their replies.** An author who answers their thread is
   an author who will notice you. This is the single best predictor of whether
   the relationship play works, and it costs one scroll.
7. **Add to this file with the count and the date you read it.** Counts move;
   an unstamped number is a guess in three weeks.

## Target roster size

**10–15 accounts, checked weekly.** Fewer than 10 and you run out of live posts
to reply to on a given day; more than 15 and you cannot actually follow what any
of them are saying, which is when replies start reading as drive-by.

Currently: **7 effective** (2 in band, 4 adjacent, plus @rawkode). The
2026-08-23 batch nominally took it to 11; screening put it back to 7. The honest
state is that this niche skews barbell, mostly sub-500 accounts and 10k+
accounts, and part of what looks like the middle is inactive. Filling it will
take a few sessions of screening rather than one.

**Where the good parents actually came from on 2026-08-23:** the searches, not
the roster. Both replies that landed on same-day posts by people describing a run
they had just done, and neither account was on this list. The roster is for
relationships; the searches are for today's slots. Do not let roster-building
substitute for running the queries.
